# To the head of compliance

Policy Atlas can help a colleague find a rule in the supplied policy book, read its exact passage, inspect its source and ask what applied on a particular date. It respects the requester’s assigned access level. When the best match is restricted, it says access is required without naming or quoting the restricted source. When evidence is insufficient, it declines to assert a rule. It cannot move money or change a customer account.

The delivered application runs locally with a tested budget-model integration and meaning-based retrieval. The latest development run answers 117 of 119 answerable questions; the published semantic judge marks 103 of 119 correct. All six known out-of-book questions are refused, and 20 of 21 blocked cases receive the correct access refusal. Every emitted quote passes the source checker, while semantic citation support is 94.25%. Overall accuracy and input usage still miss the challenge targets, and one blocked question is answered. The focused paraphrase test improved from 11/20 to 20/20, but overall development accuracy is lower than the preceding 105/119 run. These small samples are not a certification. The no-key preview remains deliberately conservative. This is not ready for unsupervised frontline decision making.

A wrong answer can cause an unauthorized waiver, an incorrect collection obligation, a missed deadline or disclosure of restricted policy. The customer and servicing team bear the direct consequences; compliance must manage the resulting remediation. The most important residual risk is a plausible interpretation supported by a merely related passage. A second risk is telling someone evidence is absent when access is actually required. Route ambiguous, disputed, unsupported or access-blocked questions to a qualified policy owner. Keep the source visible whenever a person acts on an answer.

The system identifies the version of its indexed book and when that index was loaded. It checks hashes and explicit supersession relationships on startup/reload and prevents a pending answer from slipping through a completed update. It does not monitor the regulator’s website or know that a document outside the supplied corpus changed. An old corpus loaded today is still an old corpus. Maintaining freshness requires an accountable person and an approved source-update process.

For a year of operation, assign a policy owner and an engineering owner. The policy owner reviews each amendment/withdrawal, role label, effective date and relationship to older rules; signs off the changed passages; and contributes before/after/historical regression questions. The engineering owner validates and stages the corpus, runs the regression and semantic evaluation suite, rolls it out atomically, checks access changes, and externally anchors audit receipts. Review false refusals and user-reported errors regularly, rehearse restores periodically, and review credentials, retention and model-pin availability. The actual work rate depends on source-change volume; this prototype does not justify a fabricated annual cost estimate.

Pause automated advice if any restricted information appears in an output, a current answer cites a superseded rule, source checks fail, audit integrity fails, an essential source update misses its agreed intake deadline, or semantic performance fails the agreed slice thresholds. Roll back only to an explicitly approved corpus whose rules are still applicable; rolling back code does not authorize reviving a withdrawn rule. Provider outages should show service unavailability, not a false “no such rule” answer.

Before rollout, address overall answer accuracy, the remaining blocked-question answer and the input-token budget; complete semantic access review, the private update-pack run and independently metered latency/cost checks. Approve the embedding provider for the source corpus: indexing sends source text to that provider, even though requester-visible evidence remains role-filtered. Optional calculation receipts that cannot be checked are withheld with a review warning; numeric interpretations still require review. The available evidence supports a useful searchable policy desk and tested infrastructure boundaries. It does not yet support the challenge's qualification claim or unsupervised compliance decisions.
